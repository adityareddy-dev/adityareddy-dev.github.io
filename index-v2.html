The average says everything is fine.

Most monitoring reports the mean. The mean is comfortable. The mean is also not where your users live.

Averages hide the users who are waiting.

The distribution has a tail, and the tail is where the experience actually happens. p95 says someone is hurting. p99 says who.

So I measure the tail.

At the boundary, where the user is standing. Not inside the component, where everything always looks fast.

One keystroke. 300 re-renders.

Rewrite the state layer. One.

37% faster in the field.

A form-heavy enterprise app cloned its whole state tree on every keystroke. Every cell below is a component re-rendering.

Same keystroke, new store. Only the component that changed does any work. Instrumented, not estimated.

Interaction to Next Paint, 426ms down to 268ms average. And a controlled experiment proved which change did it.

The seam between the code and the platform.

Right now I lead observability for a major application rebuild at a Fortune 100 financial services company. The legacy and rebuilt stacks run side by side, and the monitoring has to referee between them fairly or the comparison is worthless. I defined the SLI framework the stakeholders signed off on, and moved measurement to the gateway instead of trusting what each application reports about itself.

Before that I was a core engineer on straight-through processing of insurance applications: 16 Node microservices, 3 React apps, an event-driven architecture on AWS. I took the team's DevSecOps role cause someone had to, and it turned out I liked the pipelines as much as the product.

Nine years in software, seven of them building and operating production web systems in the US, across financial services, healthcare and retail supply chain.
